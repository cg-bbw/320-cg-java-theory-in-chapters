== Check Your Understanding

. Why is `Car` to `Engine` modeled as composition in this example? +
Our code doesn't intend on ripping the engine out of the car and using it separately.
. Why is `Team` to `Player` modeled as aggregation? +
The composition (ironic word choice) of the team can change.
. Does Java enforce the difference between aggregation and composition? +
No, there is no such keyword or other hard line.
. What does unidirectional mean? +
Only one object holds the reference to the other (e.g. singly linked list instead of doubly)
. What does multiplicity describe? +
How many elements can reference how many, like cardinality in relational database systems.

== Classify the Relationship

For each situation, choose the most suitable modeling term and explain your reasoning:

. A `House` creates and owns its `Room` objects. +
Composition, the Room can't exist without the House or in a different one too.
. A `Playlist` stores references to existing `Song` objects. +
Aggregation, they can exist without a Playlist or in multiple ones.
("existing" is a good indicator)
. A `Doctor` treats a `Patient` during an appointment. +
Aggregation, since the Patient can change their Doctor +
Assuming Patient or Doctor reference the other (like with a family physician) if not there wouldn't be one since the Patient is passed to a Method and only used there.
